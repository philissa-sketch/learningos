How an Academy begins

About twenty-five minutes of setup, plus three free diagnostics you run separately.

STEP 01

Answer some questions

Their goal. How many days a week. How long they can focus before it stops working. Whether they read instructions themselves or need them read aloud.

STEP 02

Measure — don't guess

Reading, math, and science diagnostics you run for free. You enter the real results. No child is ever placed on an assumption about their age.

STEP 03

Their Academy opens

Their name, their colors, their guide, their timetable, and a first quarter built against your state's requirements. Same afternoon.

A grade level is not a reading level

A nine-year-old can sit in fourth grade and read at a second-grade level. Hand her fourth-grade text and she stalls — not because she can't learn, but because nobody measured. That gap is the reason this exists, and it's why a program sold by grade can't serve her.

Placed by measurementEach subject opens at the level its weakest strand supports — never an average, never an age.
Read-aloud is recordedA level reached by listening isn't the same claim as one reached by reading. The record says which.
A guide, not a mascotEach Academy has its own character who greets, explains, and never tells a child their level.
Earned, not boughtRanks and rewards come from work finished, on a ladder named for where the child is headed.

Separate records

Each Academy keeps its own database. One child's attendance can't reach another's, even by accident during a transfer.

Separate curriculum

Lessons live in their own folder per Academy. A child only ever loads the school that belongs to them.

Nothing before sign-in

No names, ages, goals, or schedules appear on this page. A child's information exists only after that child signs in.

Records that hold up

Attendance, instructional hours, grades, portfolio, and an annual progress report — kept as you go, in the format your state expects. Pick your state and the requirements follow.

Days and hours, counted automaticallyEvery finished block adds to the ledger — including the garden, the instrument, and the field trip.
Your state's rules, not oursNotification deadlines, required subjects, testing cadence and report format come from the state you choose.
Verified, or marked unverifiedEvery state's requirements carry a source and a date checked. Anything unconfirmed says so instead of guessing.
